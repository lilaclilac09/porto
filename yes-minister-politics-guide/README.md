# Yes Minister / Yes, Prime Minister — A Politics Playbook

> *"Politics is about surviving until next week."* — Sir Humphrey Appleby

A private reference for understanding and playing politics the way it is satirised in
the BBC series **Yes Minister** (1980–1984) and **Yes, Prime Minister** (1986–1988),
written by Antony Jay and Jonathan Lynn.

This is two guides in one:

1. A **practical playbook** — the moves, framings, and stalling tactics, written so you
   can re-open it any time you need to know *how to play*.
2. An **analysis of the show** — how those moves actually appear on screen, the themes,
   and every recurring character broken down.

## The interactive interface

Open [`index.html`](index.html) in any browser (no install, works offline). It's an
interactive **adviser**: pick what you want to do and it tells you the move, plus
searchable tabs for tactics, every character, and the glossary.

## How to use this guide

- Want it interactive? Open **[`index.html`](index.html)**.
- Need a move right now? Jump to the **cheat-sheet table** at the bottom of
  [`docs/playbook.md`](docs/playbook.md).
- Want to understand the *why* behind a tactic? Read [`docs/show-analysis.md`](docs/show-analysis.md).
- Want to know who plays what game? See [`docs/characters.md`](docs/characters.md).
- Confused by the euphemisms? See [`docs/glossary.md`](docs/glossary.md).

## A word of warning

This is satire. The series is a comedy about the gap between elected ministers who *want*
to do things and a permanent civil service that *prefers things stay as they are*. The
"tactics" below are funny precisely because they describe how good intentions get quietly
neutralised. Use the playbook to **recognise** these moves being played on you as much as
to play them yourself.
