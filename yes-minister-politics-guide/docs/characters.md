# Characters — Who Plays Which Game

Every recurring character in *Yes Minister* and *Yes, Prime Minister*, with their role,
allegiance (civil service vs. politics), the political game they play, and how they relate
to the others. The principals come first; supporting and recurring figures follow.

> Allegiance key: **🏛️ Civil Service** (permanent, Whitehall) · **🗳️ Political** (elected /
> appointed, Westminster) · **🤝 Outside network** (City, press, foreign, etc.)

---

## The Principals

### Jim Hacker — 🗳️ Minister, later Prime Minister
- **Role:** Cabinet Minister at the Department of Administrative Affairs (DAA); becomes PM
  in the second series.
- **The game he plays:** Visibility and survival. Hacker wants to be *seen* achieving things,
  to win headlines, and above all to be re-elected. He genuinely wants to do good — but his
  reforming energy is constantly diverted.
- **Signature tactics:** Grand announcements; chasing favourable press; periodic bursts of
  defiance when he realises he's being managed; using his special advisers and his wife as a
  reality check against Humphrey.
- **Strengths:** Democratic legitimacy, public charm, political instinct for what plays.
- **Weaknesses:** Vanity, short attention span, dependence on the machine for detail, fear of
  the press and of looking foolish — all of which Humphrey exploits.
- **Relationships:** Locked in a love-hate duel with Humphrey; relies on Bernard to navigate;
  trusts Annie and his political advisers more than the civil service.

### Sir Humphrey Appleby — 🏛️ Permanent Secretary, later Cabinet Secretary
- **Role:** Permanent Secretary of the DAA (the senior civil servant running the department);
  promoted to Cabinet Secretary (head of the Home Civil Service) in *Yes, Prime Minister*.
- **The game he plays:** Continuity and control. His goal is a smoothly, *unchangingly* run
  service — and to make sure real power stays with the permanent officials, not the
  temporary politicians.
- **Signature tactics:** The non-answer (labyrinthine sentences), strategic delay, the
  Four-Stage Strategy, controlling Hacker's information, framing the options, and burying
  threats in committees. The master of "Yes, Minister" meaning "no."
- **Strengths:** Brilliant, erudite, endlessly patient, deeply networked across Whitehall.
- **Weaknesses:** Snobbery, complacency, and a blind spot for genuine public sentiment;
  occasionally outmanoeuvred when Hacker gets leverage over him.
- **Relationships:** Adversary-and-accomplice to Hacker; mentor-tormentor to Bernard; junior
  to (then peer/rival of) Sir Arnold; part of the mandarin network.

### Bernard Woolley — 🏛️ Principal Private Secretary
- **Role:** Hacker's Principal Private Secretary — the civil servant embedded in the
  minister's private office.
- **The game he plays:** Survival through divided loyalty. By *role* he serves the minister;
  by *career* he answers to the civil service hierarchy (and thus Humphrey). He tries to be
  honest to both and is forever caught in the middle.
- **Signature tactics:** Pedantic precision (especially about grammar and literal meaning),
  quiet truth-telling, and occasionally tipping information to one side or the other when his
  conscience or career demands.
- **Strengths:** Decent, sharp, genuinely knowledgeable; the closest thing to an honest broker.
- **Weaknesses:** Caught between two masters; ambition for his own career limits how far he'll
  defy Humphrey.
- **Relationships:** The hinge of the central triangle — the link between Hacker and Humphrey.

## The Civil Service (the mandarins) — 🏛️

### Sir Arnold Robinson — Cabinet Secretary, later head of the Campaign for Freedom of Information
- **Role:** Cabinet Secretary (the most senior civil servant) in *Yes Minister*; Humphrey's
  superior and model. After retiring, he ironically heads a freedom-of-information campaign.
- **The game he plays:** The apex of the permanent establishment — protecting the service's
  collective interest above any single department's.
- **Signature tactics:** Olympian detachment; dispensing strategy to Humphrey; defending the
  mandarins' shared code. His post-retirement role lampoons how yesterday's secret-keeper
  becomes today's transparency advocate.
- **Relationships:** Humphrey's mentor and benchmark; an equal once Humphrey becomes Cabinet
  Secretary.

### Sir Frederick "Jumbo" Stewart — senior civil servant / Permanent Secretary
- **Role:** Another senior Whitehall mandarin in Humphrey's circle.
- **The game he plays:** Part of the establishment network of Permanent Secretaries who
  coordinate behind the scenes to keep the civil service line consistent across departments.
- **Signature tactics:** Backstage coordination; mutual back-scratching among the mandarins.
- **Relationships:** Peer and ally of Humphrey within the senior service.

### The wider mandarinate — recurring Permanent Secretaries and officials
- **Role:** A rotating cast of senior civil servants from other departments (Treasury, Foreign
  Office, Home Office, etc.) who appear when a cross-department battle arises.
- **The game they play:** Each defends his own department's budget and turf, but they close
  ranks against politicians when the service's collective interest is threatened.
- **Signature tactics:** Interdepartmental committees, coordinated foot-dragging, the
  "official line."
- **Relationships:** Sometimes Humphrey's rivals over turf, always his allies against
  ministerial interference.

## The Political Side — 🗳️

### Annie Hacker — Jim's wife
- **Role:** Hacker's spouse and unofficial sounding-board.
- **The game she plays:** Honest broker at home. She has no formal power but plenty of common
  sense, and she punctures Jim's self-importance and sees through Humphrey's flattery.
- **Signature tactics:** Blunt truth-telling, moral reality-checks, dry scepticism.
- **Relationships:** Jim's most trusted (and least self-interested) adviser; an outsider to
  the Whitehall game and therefore able to name what's really going on.

### Dorothy Wainwright — Special Political Adviser (*Yes, Prime Minister*)
- **Role:** Hacker's special political adviser at Number 10.
- **The game she plays:** The political counterweight to the civil service — she gives Hacker
  advice in *his* interest rather than the machine's, and she is one of the few who can
  out-think Humphrey.
- **Signature tactics:** Spotting Humphrey's manoeuvres early and warning Hacker; supplying
  political (rather than administrative) options.
- **Strengths:** Shrewd, loyal to Hacker, unimpressed by mandarin mystique.
- **Relationships:** Humphrey's most effective rival for Hacker's ear; a genuine threat to his
  control, which is why he tries to sideline her.

### Frank Weisel — political adviser (*Yes Minister*)
- **Role:** Hacker's political adviser early in the first series.
- **The game he plays:** The outsider trying to keep Hacker's reforming agenda alive against
  the civil service.
- **Signature tactics:** Suspicion of officials, pushing Hacker to fight — but he is steadily
  neutralised by the machine (denied information, denied a proper office, eased out).
- **Relationships:** Distrusted and outmanoeuvred by Humphrey; an early casualty showing how
  the service absorbs political outsiders.

## Outside the Service — 🤝

### Sir Desmond Glazebrook — banker / City figure
- **Role:** A senior City banker who moves in the same establishment circles.
- **The game he plays:** The City's interface with Whitehall — favours, appointments, and the
  quiet alignment of financial and government interests. Notably not very bright, which is part
  of the joke about how such men rise.
- **Signature tactics:** Trading on connections; representing City interests in government
  deliberations.
- **Relationships:** Part of the establishment network around Humphrey; useful when money or
  the City is involved.

### The Prime Minister (off-screen, *Yes Minister* era)
- **Role:** The unseen PM above Hacker during the first series — a constant looming presence
  invoked but never shown.
- **The game played around him:** Both Hacker and Humphrey manoeuvre with one eye on "what
  Number 10 wants." The PM's (and the PM's office's) preferences are a lever each side uses.
- **Note:** This vacancy is what Hacker eventually fills, becoming PM himself — a promotion the
  mandarins quietly engineer because they judge him manageable.

### Party officials, the Chief Whip, and Number 10 staff
- **Role:** The party machinery — whips, party chairmen, and political fixers who appear around
  reshuffles, votes, and leadership questions.
- **The game they play:** Party discipline and electoral calculation — keeping the
  parliamentary party in line and the government in power.
- **Signature tactics:** Pressure, patronage, and the implicit threat of the reshuffle.
- **Relationships:** A third force alongside the civil service and the minister's own office —
  Hacker must satisfy them to survive.

### The press and broadcasters
- **Role:** Journalists, editors, and interviewers who appear whenever a story breaks or a leak
  is floated.
- **The game they play:** Chasing the story; being used by, and using, both politicians and
  officials.
- **Signature tactics:** The hostile interview, the leaked exclusive, the timed headline.
- **Relationships:** A tool and a threat to everyone — Hacker courts them, Humphrey
  manipulates them, and both fear them.
